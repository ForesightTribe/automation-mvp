ZEPTO DARK STORE DISCOVERY — HOW IT WORKS
=========================================

How 1,129 known stores became 1,229 verified ones across 58 cities, and why each
design decision was made. Written from what the runs actually showed, including
the approaches that failed.

1. The problem
--------------

Zepto never publishes a store directory. The only way to learn a store exists is
to ask "which store serves this point?" and read the answer.

That single fact drives everything below.

2. The three APIs
-----------------

All are https://bff-gateway.zepto.com.

  Endpoint                                          Direction      Returns
  ------------------------------------------------  -------------  ----------------------------------
  GET /api/v1/maps/place/autocomplete/?place_name=  text → places  area suggestions, place_id
  GET /api/v1/maps/place/details/?place_id=         place → point  lat, lng, city, state
  GET /lms/api/v2/get_page?latitude=&longitude=     point → store  storeId, storeDetailsResponse.name

There is no reverse endpoint — nothing maps a store back to a pincode. That
asymmetry explains most of the awkwardness later on.

get_page response, trimmed:

{
  "storeServiceableResponse": {
      "storeId": "ac3f4c36-1bc5-4305-b093-f3100f7877ed",
      "serviceable": true,
      "secondaryStoreIds": ["..."]
  },
  "storeDetailsResponse": {
      "name": "BEL-Khanapur Road",
      "city": { "name": "Belgavi", "state": "Karnataka" }
  }
}

storeDetailsResponse.name is Zepto's own label and carries a city prefix
(BLR-, GGN-, HBL-). That prefix becomes the attribution key in §7.

3. Phase 1 — the pincode method
-------------------------------

pincode
  └─ autocomplete("560035")        -> ~7 area suggestions
  └─ autocomplete("Kodathi 560035")   (label variant, more coverage)
        └─ for each place_id:
             place/details          -> lat, lng
             reject if outside the city box
             get_page(lat, lng)     -> storeId

Records the pincode and the autosuggested area text alongside the store, so
those columns come free.

Its weakness: it can only reach places autocomplete chooses to return.
Pincode 560035 holds four dark stores (Bellandur-2, Carmelaram,
Doddakannelli, Junnasandra). Seven suggestions land on three of them; the fourth
is unreachable by any pincode search.

Measured: 4,279 pincodes across 58 cities found ~1,100 stores and missed ~130.

4. Phase 2 — the grid
---------------------

for every lat/lng node in the city box, spaced 0.006° (~666 m):
    get_page(lat, lng) -> storeId

Exhaustive rather than suggestion-dependent. Nothing can hide between samples.

Why 666 m. A store serves a ~2–3 km radius, so each store is hit 9–20 times
(visible in hit_count: Karnal's two stores were hit 36 and 40 times). That is
deliberate oversampling. At 1 km spacing BLR-Bellandur 6 was never the
primary store at any node and was missed entirely; at 666 m every previously
known store was re-found (Known stores not hit by grid = 0 in all 58 cities).

Halving again quadruples cost for no observed gain.

Dedup within a scan — found is keyed on store_id; repeat hits only
increment hit_count.

5. Why both phases
------------------

Neither is sufficient, and which one wins varies by city:

  City        Phase 1 found  Phase 2 added
  ----------  -------------  ----------------------------------------------------------------
  Mumbai      119            +21 (Kalyan, Badlapur, Panvel, Virar — outside the pincode list)
  Lucknow     26             +6 (Gomti Nagar, IIM — only 24 pincodes configured)
  Delhi NCR   224            +44
  Belagavi    3              +2
  Coimbatore  9              +0 (137 pincodes for 9 stores — already saturated)

Rule of thumb: **the grid pays off where the pincode list under-covers the
city's footprint.** Cities with more pincodes than stores gain nothing.

Bengaluru showed the split is not even stable over time — the same 118 pincodes
returned 134 stores one day and 144 two days later, with the grid's contribution
falling by exactly the same amount. The total was identical both times.
Neither method is reliable alone; together they converge.

6. Deduplication — three layers
-------------------------------

The central worry: does re-finding a store inflate the count? No, at three levels.

Layer 1 — within one scan. found[store_id]; a store hit 40 times is one row.

Layer 2 — across cities. is_new compares against all 1,129 known IDs,
not just the city being scanned:

is_new = store_id not in known      # every city, not one

Hosur's box overlaps Bengaluru so heavily that its scan hit **140 stores, 139 of
them Bengaluru's**. All came back is_new = False with already_in = Bengaluru,
adding nothing. Per-city dedup would have inflated the total by 139 from that one
scan.

Layer 3 — the merge. Each city file lists everything that scan saw, so
BLR-Nagdevanahalli appears in both Bengaluru's and Hosur's file. The merge keys
on store_id and keeps one row, filed under its own city:

1,442 rows across 58 files
      ↓ key on store_id
1,234 unique store_ids

    A store is discovered as often as the grid hits it.
    A store is counted exactly once.

7. City attribution
-------------------

A store found in a shared region must belong to exactly one city.

1. Name prefix wins — BLR- → Bengaluru, KUR- → Ambala, GGN- → Delhi NCR.
   64 mappings derived from the data itself (a prefix is trusted at ≥80%
   agreement). This is Zepto's own label, and it already encodes the client's
   "covered in" taxonomy.
2. Nearest city centroid only when the prefix is unknown.

An assigned_by column records which rule fired on every row.

Two prefixes needed an explicit override. In the Chandigarh cluster the
cities sit 9–15 km apart, so the original pincode scrapes cross-contaminated each
other and SAS/PNK ended up split across four cities — below the 80%
threshold, so they fell through to nearest-centroid, which sent four SAS-
stores to Panchkula:

PREFIX_OVERRIDE = {"SAS": "Mohali",     # SAS Nagar IS Mohali
                   "PNK": "Panchkula"}

Nine misfiled stores were corrected this way, each predicted from the prefix
analysis before its scan ran, and each confirmed by it:

  Store                      Was filed           Belongs to
  -------------------------  ------------------  ----------
  HBL-Vidhya Nagar           Belagavi            Hubballi
  BLR-Mico layout network-2  Hosur               Bengaluru
  LKO-Aminabad               Kanpur              Lucknow
  LDH-Hargobind Nagar        Jalandhar           Ludhiana
  MRT-Mangal Pandey Nagar    Delhi NCR           Meerut
  GGN-Daruherra              Rewari              Delhi NCR
  VAD-Panchvati              Surat               Vadodara
  KTU-Dadabari               Jaipur              Kota
  SAS × 4                    Ambala / Panchkula  Mohali

These are re-attributions, not deletions — the national total is unaffected;
only per-city composition changes.

8. Sizing each city's box
-------------------------

box = span of that city's stores + 13 km padding, minimum ±14 km

Outliers excluded first. A store more than 45 km from its city's median is
misfiled and must not stretch the box. Jaipur held one store in Kota and one in
Mathura (199 km away), which inflated its box to 260 km across:

Jaipur  158,608 grid points (109 min)  →  4,891 points (3.4 min)
Surat    34,299                        →  6,111

Almost all of the excluded area was empty countryside.

Minimum ±14 km so single-store cities (Bhiwadi, Hisar, Mehsana, Sonipat,
Tumkuru, CSN) still get a real search area — their span is zero.

Boxes are deliberately generous, and overlap is allowed. Shrinking a box to
avoid re-scanning a neighbour risks missing stores, and Layer 2 makes the overlap
harmless. Only a box ~100% covered by a completed scan can be skipped safely;
partial overlap is not grounds to skip.

Edge check. After each scan, report how far the outermost store sits from
each edge. Under 5 km means that edge probably cut something off. Chennai's
original box left only 4.4 km below its southernmost store; widening it
produced all 6 of its new stores, every one in the strip that would have been cut.

9. Phantom stores
-----------------

Some coordinates return a storeId with no storeDetailsResponse at all:

"storeServiceableResponse": { "storeId": "ce0f9850…", "serviceable": false }
                             ← storeDetailsResponse ABSENT

Not operational stores. Reading only storeId counted them as discoveries —
Dehradun reported 2 new stores with blank names before this was caught.

Fix: capture both signals, flag rather than delete.

TOTAL unique storeIds hit             13
  real stores (have a store record)   11
  no store record (serviceable=False)  2
NEW real stores (count these)          0

5 phantoms nationally, listed in an Excluded sheet so the exclusion is
visible rather than silent.

10. Pincodes for grid-discovered stores
---------------------------------------

Grid stores have no pincode — a coordinate probe never involves one. Those 128
were filled by reverse geocoding (OpenStreetMap Nominatim, 1 req/s), and a
pincode_source column records provenance.

The two values answer different questions:

  Source                 Question                                Round-trips?
  ---------------------  --------------------------------------  --------------------
  scraper (1,101)        Which pincode search found this store?  Yes, by construction
  reverse_geocode (127)  Which pincode does this store sit in?   No

A geocoded pincode is correct as a location label but will not lead back to its
store, because one pincode holds many stores — 271 of 807 pincodes hold more than
one, and 560037 holds eight. Both statements are true at once; that is why the
column exists rather than blending them.

11. Rate limits
---------------

get_page and search have separate budgets. 256,000 grid probes ran with
zero failures, while search was blocked at the same moment.

Search enforces a volume limit — roughly 300–350 per window — not merely a
rate limit. Four approaches were measured against it:

  Approach                                                 Result
  -------------------------------------------------------  ----------
  Rotate captured session headers                          22.0% loss
  Tear down browser context for a new device_id + cookies  22.5% loss
  Pace at 1.4 s                                            2.9% loss
  Pace at 3.0 s (cold IP)                                  0%

Rotation fails because the limit tracks the IP, not the session or the client
identity — failures resumed on the very first store after every rotation.

Zepto signals throttling with HTTP 299, a non-standard code. Treating it as
an empty result meant retrying three times into an active block, which prolonged
it.

Pacing helps but cannot eliminate a volume cap: 12 s spacing got 34 stores per
window versus 5 at 5 s — 6.5× more — but the wall still arrives.

12. Results
-----------

1,129  →  1,229 unique dark stores   (+100, +8.9%)

  City       Before  After
  ---------  ------  -----
  Delhi NCR  214     266
  Bengaluru  168     169
  Mumbai     115     140
  Hyderabad  130     130
  Chennai    93      94
  Pune       59      59
  Kolkata    53      53
  Lucknow    25      32
  Mohali     5       14
  Ambala     7       2

Ambala's fall and Mohali's rise are the same event: SAS- and KUR- stores
moving to their correct cities.

Where the gains came from. Delhi NCR and Mumbai supplied 78 of the first 101
new stores. Both are large and had never been grid-scanned, with big suburban
belts outside their pincode lists. Small cities where the pincode list already
covers the footprint returned 0–2 each.

14. Verification
----------------

Every coordinate in a city file can be sent back to get_page to confirm it
returns the store the file claims. Kolkata: 51/53 exact (96.2%).

Both exceptions were catchment-boundary cases — the two stores sat 0.85 km and
3.35 km apart in dense New Town, and Zepto reassigned the boundary in the ~30
minutes between scrape and check. Not bad data.

Coordinates are not store addresses. Zepto never publishes those. Every
coordinate here means "a point this store serves". That is why a store's
coordinate can differ by a few km between runs, and why 32 of Kolkata's 53
differed from the master by up to 3.87 km — both values are valid points inside
the same catchment.
